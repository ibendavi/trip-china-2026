China 2026 — The Grand Tour

Hong Kong → Guizhou → Gannan → Silk Road → Beijing → Shenzhen

Itzhak & Romy · Aug 26 – Sep 23, 2026 · v2, rebuilt around a Hong Kong gateway and an 18-day adventure leg

What changed in v2, and why

1 · Fly in and out of Hong Kong, not Beijing. This is the whole unlock. Transpacific flights to Beijing all leave the US around midday and land in the evening, so departing Columbus on the night of Aug 26 buys you nothing. Flights to Hong Kong leave the US after midnight and land at about 06:30 — so a Wednesday-evening CMH departure puts you on the ground in Asia the morning of Fri Aug 28 instead of that night. That is a full extra day. Hong Kong is also where the trip ends: Shenzhen is twenty minutes from Hong Kong West Kowloon, so one round trip covers both ends and matches PHBS's "to or from Shenzhen or Hong Kong" reimbursement rule.

2 · Enter overland, and delete a flight. From Hong Kong West Kowloon a single high-speed train runs into Guizhou on the Guangzhou–Guiyang line, stopping at Congjiang — about four hours, and Zhaoxing is half an hour from the station. That removes the Beijing→Guiyang flight entirely and turns the arrival day into a productive travel day instead of a wasted evening.

3 · The extra day kills the worst drive. v1's Day 13 was seven hours of pure transit from Zhagana to Lanzhou. It is now split: a night in Linxia, the Hui Muslim city at the edge of the plateau, then Bingling Temple — cliff-carved Buddhist grottoes reached by boat on the Yellow River — on the way into Lanzhou. A bad day became two good ones.

4 · Same landing point. You are still in Beijing the evening of Sep 14, with Sep 15 clear before the Tsinghua seminar on Sep 16. Nothing on the work side moved.

The tickets, in booking order

  1. International — CMH ⇄ HKG, one round trip. Out the evening of Wed Aug 26; back Wed Sep 23. Cathay's JFK departure leaves ~01:35 and lands HKG 06:30; SFO and LAX have ~00:05–00:30 departures landing about 06:00. Book it round-trip into and out of Hong Kong rather than open-jaw — cheaper, and it fits the PHBS reimbursement rule. Keep the fare changeable until the passport is physically back.
  2. High-speed rail — Hong Kong West Kowloon → Congjiang, Fri Aug 28. The three daily West Kowloon → Guiyang G-trains leave at 10:02, 11:14 and 11:52 and run over the Guangzhou–Guiyang line, on which both Congjiang and Rongjiang are stations. Book West Kowloon → Congjiang directly and confirm your train actually stops there. Take the 11:14 or 11:52 — not the 10:02, which is too tight against a 06:30 landing. Mainland immigration is co-located at West Kowloon, so you clear China before boarding. Chinese rail normally opens booking about 15 days out, so this one waits until roughly Aug 13.
  3. Guiyang (KWE) → Lanzhou (LHW), Fri Sep 4, morning. 1h35m, and the hinge of the entire trip. Book early and do not take the last departure of the day.
  4. Rail Lanzhou → Zhangye, Fri Sep 11, midday. About 3h on the Lanzhou–Xinjiang line.
  5. Zhangye → Dunhuang, Sat Sep 12. Roughly 5h by rail or a short flight. Ask the Gansu operator to price both against that specific date rather than deciding now.
  6. Dunhuang (DNH) → Beijing, Mon Sep 14, afternoon. Direct where the season supports it, otherwise via Xi'an or Lanzhou. Take the earliest afternoon option that still leaves the 9am Mogao tour intact.
  7. Beijing → Shenzhen (SZX), Sat Sep 19.
  8. Sep 23 exit. Futian → Hong Kong West Kowloon by rail (~20 min), or the Shekou → HKG SkyPier ferry, which reaches the airport without formally entering Hong Kong. Either route stamps your US passport out of the mainland — and those stamp pages are what Tsinghua needs before the honorarium moves.

Five things that can actually break this

1 · The passport. It is with the visa agent (ServiceIn/CEV, invoice #687441) against a target return of ~Aug 20. An Aug 26 departure leaves six days of margin. Call, confirm that date against Aug 26, and price the expedite. As a US citizen you must re-enter the United States on the US passport, so the Greek-passport fallback does not remove this dependency — nothing here is bookable with confidence until it is in your hand.

2 · What the visa actually says when it arrives. Check three fields before you confirm any ticket: the enter before date is on or before Aug 28; the duration of stay per entry covers Aug 28 → Sep 23, which is 26 days; and it is the multiple-entry F. Separately, the signed Tsinghua letter covers Sep 1–30 and names Beijing only, and you now enter three days early at a rail port in the south. That is normally a non-issue on an issued F visa, but carry the letter and this itinerary printed.

3 · Romy's visa-free entry at West Kowloon. She enters on the Greek passport under China's unilateral visa-free scheme. Confirm before you book the train that the scheme is honoured at the Hong Kong West Kowloon mainland port for a Greek passport — some entry schemes are port-restricted, and that would be an expensive thing to discover at the barrier. If the answer is anything short of a clear yes, fly HKG → Guiyang instead (about 2h, several daily) and pick up the rail to Congjiang from Guiyang; the day still ends in Zhaoxing. Her clock either way: in Aug 28, out Sep 23, 26 days against a 30-day limit. Do not extend the tail.

4 · Mogao tickets. Foreigners cannot book through the WeChat mini-program. The foreigner allocation is sold in person at the Digital Exhibition Centre, about 9 km outside Dunhuang. Get it in writing from the operator: how, and when, they will secure the 9am English tour on the ¥258 "A" ticket.

5 · Rain. September closes mountain roads in both Guizhou and Gannan occasionally. Day 6 (Getu River) and Day 17 (western desert) are the two days to sacrifice if it comes to that. Everything else is load-bearing.

The shape of it

Three acts, one internal flight, two rail legs. Act I is subtropical village China — Dong and Miao, indigo, sour fish soup, karst. Act II is the Tibetan plateau at its least-visited: Labrang, Langmusi, the rock towers of Zhagana, then down through Hui Linxia to the Yellow River grottoes. Act III is high desert — the rainbow Danxia, the Silk Road's western passes, and the Mogao caves.

Act I  Days 1–7 · Guizhou · in by rail from Hong Kong, loop east then west to Guiyang

Act II  Days 8–14 · Gannan · fly Guiyang→Lanzhou (1h35m), then a driving loop out and back

Act III  Days 15–18 · Silk Road · rail to Zhangye, on to Dunhuang, fly out

One hard transfer day remains — Day 4, Rongjiang→Leishan, about four hours of mountain road. That is the price of reaching places without airports. Everything else is a comfortable half-day or less. The plan still has little padding: treat Days 6 and 17 as the flexible ones.

Act I · Guizhou — villages, indigo, and karst

Days 1–7 · Aug 28 – Sep 3 · Private guide-interpreter and driver throughout. Nights are a mix of village guesthouses and simple county-town hotels — this is the low-amenity stretch of the trip, and the one where a guide matters most.

Day 1Fri Aug 28Off the plane and onto the rails

Land at HKG around 06:30 off the overnight transpacific. Airport Express to Kowloon, then Hong Kong West Kowloon station and the 11:14 train straight into Guizhou — mainland immigration is co-located at the station, so you clear China standing still. Congjiang in about four hours, and Zhaoxing is half an hour from the platform. Zhaoxing is the largest Dong village in China: five drum towers, one for each clan, and wind-and-rain bridges over the stream running through the middle. It has become genuinely touristy, so treat it as a comfortable base rather than the destination — the quiet is up the hill tomorrow. Sleep early and hard.

MoveHKG 06:30 · West Kowloon→Congjiang ~4h · drive ~30 minNightZhaoxing

See itZhaoxing Dong Village·Photo-heavy guide·MTR high-speed destinations

Day 2Sat Aug 29Tang'an terraces on foot

Hike the old stone path from Zhaoxing up to Tang'an, a small terraced Dong hamlet on the ridge above — roughly two hours up through rice terraces, with the valley opening behind you the whole way. Tang'an is an open-air ecomuseum and much quieter than Zhaoxing. Walk back down in the afternoon, or continue along the ridge if the weather is good. Evening: Dong polyphonic choir singing drifts through the village after dark, unamplified and unstaged if you find the right courtyard.

MoveWalking day, ~10–14 kmNightZhaoxing

See itTang'an & the quiet villages·Zhaoxing area overview

Day 3Sun Aug 30Basha, and west to Rongjiang

Morning at Basha — the Miao village whose men still carry matchlock rifles, the only civilian community in China licensed to do so, and who wear hand-woven hemp dyed with tree bark. The gunfire-and-lusheng welcome is arranged per group rather than run to a public timetable, so have the guide fix it in advance, and go early before the coaches. Afternoon: on to Rongjiang and the small Dong village of Zhanli, cut off enough that it still has its own customs.

MoveCongjiang→Basha ~30 min · on to Rongjiang ~1.5hNightRongjiang or Dali

Day 4Mon Aug 31Dali indigo, then the long road north

Morning in Dali Dong village on the Duliu River — six hundred years old, wooden balconies over the stream, and the indigo tradition that defines the place: women harvest the leaves from the hillsides, ferment them into paste, and dye cloth that takes about a year to finish a single bolt. Then the trip's one hard drive, north into the Leigong mountains, arriving at Langde in the afternoon for the long-skirt Miao village and its silver work.

Day 5Tue Sep 1Leigongshan ridges

A full day walking the ridges that connect the Miao hamlets above Langde — the kind of hiking where you pass through three villages and see no other outsiders. Lunch is sour fish soup, ideally cooked in a hot stone pot: fermented rice water and tomato, sharp and clean, and the dish the whole province is built around. Afternoon with a silversmith or blacksmith if the guide can arrange it — Leishan is the centre of Miao silver work.

Day 6Wed Sep 2Getu River karst

Drive west out of the Miao mountains to the Getu River, where the karst stops being scenery and becomes architecture: the Great Arch is one of the largest natural rock spans anywhere, with a cave system beneath it and a river running through. Local climbers free-climb the arch face. Boat in, walk the caves, and see the karst without the Yangshuo crowds. This is one of your two flexible days — if rain has cost you something earlier, spend it here.

MoveLeishan→Getuhe ~4–5hNightGetuhe or Anshun

See itGetu River scenic area

Day 7Thu Sep 3Qingyan, and the food night

Back toward Guiyang, stopping at Qingyan — a Ming-dynasty walled garrison town thirty kilometres south of the city, and the right place to end Act I because it is a genuine eating destination. The list: Qingyan pig's trotters simmered with ginger, rice tofu, tofu balls, siwawa (vegetables rolled in a thin pancake), and a bowl of changwang noodles. Stay into the evening — the coach crowds leave at dusk and the lanes are worth walking after dark. Sleep near the airport; tomorrow starts early.

Act II · Gannan — the Tibetan plateau, without the permits

Days 8–14 · Sep 4 – Sep 10 · A second operator takes over here. This is Amdo Tibetan country in southern Gansu — no permits needed, unlike the Tibet Autonomous Region, and very few foreign visitors. Altitude runs roughly 2,900–3,000 m, high enough to notice on the first day but not high enough to need an acclimatization plan. The act now exits through Hui Linxia rather than in one seven-hour push.

Day 8Fri Sep 4North-west, to south of the Yellow River

Fly Guiyang to Lanzhou in the morning — the hinge of the whole trip, and mercifully short. From Lanzhou, drive south-west into the grasslands to Xiahe. The landscape changes completely within two hours: terraced green gives way to open high pasture, and the prayer flags start. Arrive in time for the evening kora, when the pilgrims walk.

MoveGuiyang→Lanzhou 1h35m · drive to Xiahe ~3h (220 km)NightXiahe

See itXiahe & Labrang·Gannan overview

Day 9Sat Sep 5Labrang

Labrang is one of the six great Gelug monasteries and, outside Tibet proper, the most important Tibetan Buddhist institution in China — founded 1709, still a working university with hundreds of monks. Its kora is 3.5 km, the longest pilgrim circuit in Tibetan Buddhism, lined with prayer wheels; walking it at dawn alongside the pilgrims is the thing to do here. Afternoon out to the Sangke grasslands, or the Ganjia basin with its octagonal walled ruin, and a visit to a Tibetan family.

MoveLocal, short drivesNightXiahe

See itLabrang Monastery·Xiahe & Sangke grassland

Day 10Sun Sep 6Langmusi, on the provincial line

Drive south to Langmusi, a small monastery town straddling the Gansu–Sichuan border — the provincial line runs down the middle, with Sertri Gompa on the Gansu side and the 600-year-old Kirti Gompa on the Sichuan side. Alpine meadows and forested ridges surround it; the comparison people reach for is rural Austria, which is not quite right but not wrong either. Gahai Lake and open grassland on the way.

Day 11Mon Sep 7Namo gorge, then into Zhagana

Morning hike up the Namo gorge behind Kirti Gompa — a narrow limestone cleft that opens into high meadow, a couple of hours up and back, and the sky-burial ground sits on the ridge above town (approach only if your guide says it is appropriate that day). Afternoon: the short drive to Zhagana, which is the reason this act exists.

MoveHike ~3h · Langmusi→Zhagana ~2hNightZhagana

Day 12Tue Sep 8Zhagana, all day

Zhagana means "stone box" in Tibetan: a natural amphitheatre of vertical limestone towers with four Tibetan villages, barley terraces and wooden houses stacked on the valley floor inside it. Three main trails climb out of the basin for the panoramic view down over the villages; take the longest one you have daylight for. This is the solitude core of the trip — almost no foreign visitors reach it, and the walking is as good as anything in the Alps with none of the traffic.

MoveFull hiking dayNightZhagana

See itZhagana trails·Gannan region guide

Day 13Wed Sep 9Down off the plateau, into Hui countrynew in v2

Out of Zhagana north through the grasslands, breaking at Hezuo, and down into Linxia — about four and a half hours instead of the seven this used to be. Linxia is the centre of Hui Muslim China, sometimes called its Little Mecca: eighty-odd mosques in the prefecture, the Bafang Shisan Xiang quarter of lanes and minarets, and a switch from Tibetan Buddhism to Islam so abrupt it reads as a different country. It is also a craft town — Linxia gourd carving, brick carving, and the Dongxiang and Bao'an minorities who live nowhere else. Eat the hand-pulled beef noodles here rather than waiting for Lanzhou.

MoveZhagana→Linxia ~4.5h via HezuoNightLinxia

See itLinxia City·Linxia travel guide

Day 14Thu Sep 10Grottoes by boat, then the citynew in v2

Drive to the Liujiaxia reservoir and take a boat across to Bingling Temple: a cliff of Buddhist caves cut over sixteen hundred years, from the Western Qin onward, with a 27-metre Maitreya carved into the rock face and a gorge of eroded pinnacles behind it. It is reachable only by water, which is a large part of why it survived the centuries that flattened easier targets — and a good primer for Mogao at the other end of the trip. On to Lanzhou in the afternoon. If there is energy left, the Gansu Provincial Museum holds the best Silk Road collection in the country: free but reservation-only, and September falls inside the window when the original Bronze Galloping Horse is on display rather than the replica. Early night; tomorrow is a rail day.

MoveLinxia→Liujiaxia ~1h · boat ~1h each way · on to Lanzhou ~2hNightLanzhou

Act III · The Silk Road — desert, colour, and the caves

Days 15–18 · Sep 11 – Sep 14 · The infrastructure gets easy here: high-speed rail between cities, day drivers for the desert sites. September is the ideal window — dry, clear, and before the October Golden Week crush. You are skipping Jiayuguan's Great Wall fort deliberately, since Romy will see the Wall properly from Beijing after Sep 15.

Day 15Fri Sep 11Rainbow rock

High-speed rail north-west to Zhangye, into the Hexi Corridor — the thin strip of oasis between desert and mountain that the entire Silk Road had to thread. Late afternoon at the Danxia landforms: sandstone laid down in stripes of red, orange, cream and green, tilted on end and stripped bare. Go late, roughly two hours before sunset when the light is low and the colour saturates; the quieter Binggou canyon nearby is the version without the crowds.

MoveLanzhou→Zhangye by rail ~3hNightZhangye

See itZhangye Danxia geopark·Photographer's guide

Day 16Sat Sep 12Dawn colour, then deeper into the desert

Back to the Danxia at opening for the dawn light and an empty park — worth the alarm, and the colours are deepest just after rain if you get lucky. Then west to Dunhuang, the last real oasis before the desert proper. Arrive late afternoon and go straight to Mingsha Shan: dunes several hundred metres high with Crescent Lake, a spring-fed pool shaped like its name, sitting in a hollow between them and somehow never buried in all the centuries it has been recorded. Climb for sunset.

MoveZhangye→Dunhuang by rail ~5h or short flight — let the operator pickNightDunhuang

See itMingsha Shan & Crescent Lake·Echoing-Sand Mountain

Day 17Sun Sep 13The western passes

A full day out into the desert west of Dunhuang, and the emptiest landscape of the trip. Yumen Pass — the Jade Gate — is a lone rammed-earth block on gravel plain, the checkpoint everything bound for Central Asia once passed through. Nearby, eroded ramparts of the Han-dynasty Great Wall, older than the Ming brickwork tourists know, built of reeds and sand. Then the Yardang geopark, wind-carved rock formations in an old lakebed that the Chinese call the Ghost City; stay for sunset if the guide is willing. Your second flexible day — this is the one to shorten if the schedule has slipped.

MoveFull-day car ~8:30am–6:30pm, no public transport out thereNightDunhuang

See itYumen Pass·Yardang geopark

Day 18Mon Sep 14Mogao, and out

The Mogao Grottoes to finish: seven hundred caves cut into a cliff over a thousand years, painted floor to ceiling, the greatest surviving repository of Buddhist art anywhere. Take the 9am English tour — the ¥258 "A" ticket covers eight caves plus the digital films. Remember foreigners cannot book this through the WeChat system; the allocation is sold in person at the Digital Exhibition Centre, so have the operator handle it days ahead. Afternoon flight to Beijing, landing in the evening.

MoveDunhuang→Beijing, afternoon/evening flightNightBeijing

What to hand the operators

Split this at Day 7. Send Act I to the Guizhou operator and Acts II–III to the Gansu operator, and ask each to price their half against it. Points to make explicit:

  • Guizhou: we want Zhanli and Dali, not Xijiang Qianhu. Confirm the driver has personally driven Rongjiang→Leishan. Ask them to pre-arrange the Basha welcome and, if possible, a silversmith visit and one village meal. Note the arrival is by rail into Congjiang, not by air into Guiyang — they need to meet the train.
  • Gannan: confirm lodging inside Zhagana rather than in Diebu town. The exit is now Zhagana→Linxia→Bingling→Lanzhou over two days, so ask them to confirm the Liujiaxia boat operates on Sep 10 and at what water level, and whether the Bingling visit is the speedboat or the slow ferry.
  • Silk Road: Mogao tickets are the one genuine booking risk — get written confirmation of how and when they will secure the English tour. Also ask them to compare rail vs. flight for Zhangye→Dunhuang on that specific date.
  • All three: what happens if rain closes a road. You want to hear a specific alternative, not reassurance.
