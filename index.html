What changed, and why top ↑

1 · Fly in and out of Hong Kong, not Beijing. This is the whole unlock. Transpacific flights to Beijing all leave the US around midday and land in the evening, so departing Columbus on the night of Aug 26 buys you nothing. Flights to Hong Kong leave the US after midnight and land at about 06:30 — so a Wednesday-evening CMH departure puts you on the ground in Asia the morning of Fri Aug 28 instead of that night. That is a full extra day. Hong Kong is also where the trip ends: Shenzhen is twenty minutes from Hong Kong West Kowloon, so one round trip covers both ends and matches PHBS's "to or from Shenzhen or Hong Kong" reimbursement rule.

2 · Enter overland, and delete a flight. From Hong Kong West Kowloon a single high-speed train runs into Guizhou on the Guangzhou–Guiyang line, stopping at Congjiang — about four hours, and Zhaoxing is half an hour from the station. That removes the Beijing→Guiyang flight entirely and turns the arrival day into a productive travel day instead of a wasted evening.

3 · The extra day kills the worst drive. The old Day 13 was seven hours of pure transit from Zhagana to Lanzhou. It is now split: a night in Linxia, the Hui Muslim city at the edge of the plateau, then Bingling Temple — cliff-carved Buddhist grottoes reached by boat on the Yellow River — on the way into Lanzhou. A bad day became two good ones.

4 · R runs her own days during the work block. Four of the nine days in Beijing and Shenzhen are seminar days. Those now have a plan for R rather than a gap, and the reservations they need are on the to-do list — several are real-name, passport-required, and sell out.

5 · Same landing point. You are still in Beijing the evening of Sep 14, with Sep 15 clear before the Tsinghua seminar on Sep 16. Nothing on the work side moved.

Five things that can break this top ↑

1 · The passport. It is with the visa agency against a target return of ~Aug 20. An Aug 26 departure leaves six days of margin. Call, confirm that date against Aug 26, and price the expedite. As a US citizen you must re-enter the United States on the US passport, so the Greek-passport fallback does not remove this dependency — nothing here is bookable with confidence until it is in hand.

2 · What the visa actually says when it arrives. Check three fields before confirming any ticket: the Enter Before date — which is the last day the visa may be used — must fall on or after Aug 28 (it normally sits ~3 months from issue, so this should be comfortable); the duration of stay per entry covers Aug 28 → Sep 23, which is 26 days; and the entries field. A single entry is actually sufficient for this route — you never leave the mainland and return — but the multiple-entry F was applied for and is what should arrive. Separately: the signed invitation letter covers Sep 1–30 and names Beijing only, while you enter Aug 28 at a southern rail port. On an issued F visa that is a non-issue — a visa is valid at any open port — but carry the letter and this itinerary printed.

3 · The Arrival Card, and the entry paperwork. Since Nov 2025 China requires every foreign visitor to file an online Arrival Card (NIA website or the 12367 app) before entry — visa-free and visa'd alike. Two minutes online; genuinely awkward at a rail port if forgotten. Both travellers, filed in the final week before departure. R's entry itself is no longer an open question: Greece's 30-day unilateral visa-free scheme is confirmed through Dec 31, 2026, applies at West Kowloon's mainland port, and permits multiple entries. Have her carry evidence of onward travel, lodging, and the family relationship; her clock is Aug 28 → Sep 23, 26 days against 30, and her Hong Kong tail (90 days visa-free for Greek citizens) is separate.

4 · Mogao tickets. Real-name, timed, capacity-capped. The peak "A" ticket is ¥238 (eight caves plus the films), English tours ~9:00/12:00/14:30, arrive 30 minutes early. 2026 reports describe an official Dunhuang Academy WeChat channel with an English path that accepts foreign passports; the in-person allocation at the Digital Exhibition Centre still exists as fallback. Either way the instruction stands: the Hexi operator secures the 9am English tour for Sep 14, in writing, days ahead — and avoid unofficial resellers entirely.

5 · Rain, and what gets dropped. September closes mountain roads in Guizhou and Gannan occasionally. The pre-agreed casualties, in order: Day 2's Tang'an hike (if the arrival slips), Day 6 (Getu), Day 17 (the western desert). Those are drop-first activities — the plan's real buffers are Sep 15 and Sep 18 in Beijing. Everything else is load-bearing.

The shape of it

Three acts, one internal flight, two rail legs. Act I is subtropical village China — Dong and Miao, indigo, sour fish soup, karst. Act II is the Tibetan plateau at its least-visited: Labrang, Langmusi, the rock towers of Zhagana, then down through Hui Linxia to the Yellow River grottoes. Act III is high desert — the rainbow Danxia, the Silk Road's western passes, and the Mogao caves.

Act I  Days 1–7 · Guizhou · in by rail from Hong Kong via Guangzhou South, loop east then west to Guiyang

Act II  Days 8–14 · Gannan · fly Guiyang→Lanzhou (~2h), then a driving loop out and back

Act III  Days 15–18 · Silk Road · rail to Zhangye, on to Dunhuang, fly out

No single brutal drive remains — the Rongjiang–Leishan expressway (opened late 2023) deleted the old four-hour mountain leg. The longest road days are now Lanzhou airport→Xiahe (~4h) and Zhagana→Linxia (~4.5h). The plan still has little padding: Getu (Day 6) and the desert loop (Day 17) are the drop-first days, and the true buffers are Sep 15 and 18.

Day 1Fri Aug 28Off the plane and onto the railstop ↑

Land at HKG between ~05:00 and 06:30 off the overnight transpacific (the JFK and BOS redeyes land ~05:00–05:30; SFO/LAX ~05:45–06:20). Airport Express to Kowloon (~22 min), then Hong Kong West Kowloon station — mainland immigration is co-located there, so you clear China before boarding; be inside security 45–60 minutes before departure. There is no direct train to Congjiang. The plan: the 11:14 G-train to Guangzhou South (arrives 12:06), transfer to a Congjiang train — about 14 run daily, the fastest take ~2h30, the last useful one leaves ~15:55. Congjiang station by mid-to-late afternoon; Zhaoxing is only ~8 km from the station (10–20 minutes — the station sits on the Zhaoxing side, not in Congjiang town, 43 km away). Two interchange variants exist and the Aug 14 timetable decides between them: Guangzhou South (arr 12:06, ~14 onward trains daily — the robust default) or Guilin West (arr ~14:20, onward ~14:59 → Congjiang 15:37 — faster but a ~39-minute connection), with Shenzhen North as a third variant if it produces a safer chain. Book whichever as one through-booking; presale opens Aug 14 under the 15-day rule (the exact release hour varies by channel — set a Trip.com pre-order 30–60 days ahead so it auto-submits the moment the window opens, and check 12306 early that morning).

The failure tree, agreed in advance: miss the 11:14 → take the 11:52 and a later Guangzhou South connection. Miss the last Congjiang train → ride to Guilin West or Guiyang North and let the driver reposition, or overnight in Guangzhou and rejoin the morning of Aug 29 — Day 2's Tang'an hike is the pre-designated casualty, nothing else moves. The operator's driver meets the train, whichever train it turns out to be.

Zhaoxing is the largest Dong village in China: five drum towers, one for each clan, and wind-and-rain bridges over the stream running through the middle. It has become genuinely touristy, so treat it as a comfortable base rather than the destination — the quiet is up the hill tomorrow. Sleep early and hard.

Day 4Mon Aug 31Dali indigo, a village market, and north on the new roadtop ↑

Morning in Dali Dong village on the Duliu River — six hundred years old, wooden balconies over the stream, and the indigo tradition that defines the place: women harvest the leaves from the hillsides, ferment them into paste, and dye cloth that takes about a year to finish a single bolt. Day 6Wed Sep 2Getu River karstdrop-firsttop ↑

Drive west out of the Miao mountains to the Getu River, where the karst stops being scenery and becomes architecture: the Great Arch is one of the largest natural rock spans anywhere, with a cave system beneath it and a river running through. Act II · Gannan — the Tibetan plateau, without the permits top ↑

Days 8–14 · Sep 4 – Sep 10 · A second operator takes over here. This is Amdo Tibetan country in southern Gansu — no permits needed, unlike the Tibet Autonomous Region, and very few foreign visitors. Altitude, honestly: Xiahe ~2,920 m, Langmusi ~3,300 m+, Zhagana's villages ~3,000–3,300 m with the recommended trails reaching ~3,500–3,900 m. That is genuine altitude-illness territory for unacclimatized travellers (the medical threshold is ~2,450 m sleeping elevation), arrived at from near sea level in a single day. The protocol, built into the contract: the first evening in Xiahe stays quiet — the kora walk and nothing more; no alcohol and no strenuous climbing in the first 48 hours; hydrate aggressively; the guide gets a symptom-based turnaround rule for the hikes; and the trip insurance carries trekking cover to ≥4,000 m. Discuss the altitude plan (and whether to carry acetazolamide) with your physician before departure. The act exits through Hui Linxia rather than in one seven-hour push.

Day 8Fri Sep 4North-west, to south of the Yellow Rivertop ↑

Fly Guiyang to Lanzhou in the morning — the hinge of the whole trip. Take the earliest departure (~08:55, landing ~11:05); block time is about two hours, and there are up to three flights a day, so a morning failure still has a same-day recovery. That is the whole evening's program, by design — you have just come up 2,000 metres, so it is an easy dinner, water, and bed.

Day 9Sat Sep 5Labrangtop ↑

Labrang is one of the six great Gelug monasteries and, outside Tibet proper, the most important Tibetan Buddhist institution in China — founded 1709, still a working university with hundreds of monks. Its 3.5 km kora is the world's longest prayer-wheel corridor — 1,700-plus wheels — and walking it at dawn alongside the pilgrims is the thing to do here — treat the dawn start as conditional on a decent first night at altitude; the kora is just as real at 9am. The monks' debating sessions (the clapping ritual) run late morning or afternoon, so the two bracket the day neatly; the guide confirms the day's times. Afternoon out to the Sangke grasslands, or the Ganjia basin with its octagonal walled ruin, and a visit to a Tibetan family.

Day 12Tue Sep 8Zhagana, all daytop ↑

Zhagana means "stone box" in Tibetan: a natural amphitheatre of vertical limestone towers with four Tibetan villages, barley terraces and wooden houses stacked on the valley floor inside it. Three main trails climb out of the basin for the panoramic view down over the villages. Take the longest one you have daylight for, and treat that as the point of the day — the valley floor and the main viewpoints are a genuine domestic-tourism draw now and will be busy, while an hour up any trail the crowd vanishes entirely. Almost no foreign visitors reach Zhagana; that is not the same as it being empty. Practicalities that are new since 2025: the scenic area now runs a 5,000-visitor daily quota booked ~3 days ahead on its WeChat mini-program (¥80 entry + ¥40 shuttle), and private cars are banned inside the basin — the operator secures the quota slots for Sep 7–8 and confirms how in-basin guests handle the shuttle and luggage. The trails top out around 3,500–3,900 m: pace accordingly, and turn around on symptoms, not summits.

Day 13Wed Sep 9Down off the plateau, into Hui countrynewtop ↑

Out of Zhagana north through the grasslands, breaking at Hezuo for the nine-storey Milarepa Tower — the only gompa of its kind in Amdo, an hour well spent and on the road anyway — and down into Linxia — about four and a half hours instead of the seven this used to be. Linxia is the centre of Hui Muslim China, sometimes called its Little Mecca: eighty-odd mosques in the prefecture, the Bafang Shisan Xiang quarter of lanes and minarets, and a switch from Tibetan Buddhism to Islam so abrupt it reads as a different country. It is also a craft town — Linxia gourd carving, brick carving, and the Dongxiang and Bao'an minorities who live nowhere else. Eat the hand-pulled beef noodles here rather than waiting for Lanzhou.
